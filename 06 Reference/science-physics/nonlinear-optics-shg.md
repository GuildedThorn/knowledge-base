# Nonlinear Optics and Second-Harmonic Generation

## Purpose

At high intensities the medium's polarization is nonlinear, enabling frequency conversion such as second-harmonic generation.

## Nonlinear Susceptibility

- At low intensity the induced polarization is linear in the field; at high intensity higher-order terms in chi(2), chi(3), and beyond matter.
- The chi(2) term drives three-wave mixing processes: second-harmonic generation, sum- and difference-frequency generation, and parametric amplification.
- chi(2) is nonzero only in non-centrosymmetric crystals; centrosymmetric media show leading chi(3) effects such as self-focusing.
- Intense coherent laser light was required before these effects became observable, which is why nonlinear optics postdates the laser.

## Second-Harmonic Generation

- Two photons of frequency omega combine in a chi(2) medium to produce one photon at 2*omega, doubling the frequency and halving the wavelength.
- First demonstrated by Franken et al. in 1961 by focusing a ruby laser into crystalline quartz.
- Conversion efficiency scales with input intensity, interaction length, and the nonlinear coefficient of the crystal.

## Phase Matching

- Efficient conversion requires the fundamental and harmonic waves to stay in phase, i.e. momentum conservation among photons.
- Birefringent phase matching uses crystal birefringence to equalize the refractive indices at the two frequencies.
- Quasi-phase-matching periodically reverses the sign of chi(2) (e.g. periodically poled lithium niobate) to reset accumulated phase slip.
