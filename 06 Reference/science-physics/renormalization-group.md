# The Renormalization Group

## Purpose

The renormalization group explains universality and critical exponents through scale transformations and flow toward fixed points.

## How It Works

- Coarse-graining integrates out short-wavelength degrees of freedom, then rescales lengths, producing an effective theory with renormalized couplings.
- Iterating this map defines RG flow in coupling space; the flow's trajectories describe how a system looks at successively larger scales.
- At a critical point the correlation length diverges, so the system is scale-invariant and sits at a fixed point of the flow.

## Fixed Points and Universality

- Near a fixed point, couplings split into relevant, irrelevant, and marginal operators according to the linearized flow eigenvalues.
- Relevant operators must be tuned (e.g. temperature, field); irrelevant ones die off, so microscopically different systems flow to the same fixed point.
- This explains universality: critical exponents depend only on dimensionality and symmetry, not microscopic detail, and are set by the fixed-point eigenvalues.
- Wilson and Kogut's epsilon expansion computes exponents perturbatively in $\epsilon = 4 - d$ about the upper critical dimension.
