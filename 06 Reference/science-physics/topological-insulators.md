# Topological Insulators

## Purpose

Topological insulators are bulk-insulating materials with symmetry-protected conducting edge or surface states set by a Z2 invariant.

## Topological Invariant

- The bulk band structure carries a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ topological invariant distinguishing trivial insulators from topological ones in time-reversal-symmetric systems.
- The invariant cannot change without closing the bulk gap, so it is robust against smooth perturbations and disorder that preserve the symmetry.
- Bulk-boundary correspondence guarantees gapless states at any interface between phases with different invariants.

## Edge and Surface States

- 2D topological insulators (quantum spin Hall systems, e.g. HgTe quantum wells) host helical edge channels with spin-momentum locking.
- 3D topological insulators (e.g. Bi$_2$Se$_3$, Bi$_2$Te$_3$) have surface states forming a single Dirac cone.
- These states are protected against backscattering from nonmagnetic impurities because reversing momentum would require flipping spin.

## Physical Origin

- Strong spin-orbit coupling drives band inversion, exchanging the usual ordering of conduction and valence bands.
- This inversion is what changes the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant relative to the vacuum, forcing conducting boundary modes.
- Thouless, Haldane, and Kosterlitz shared the 2016 Nobel Prize for topological phases and phase transitions of matter.
