# Bloch's Theorem and Electronic Band Theory

## Purpose

Bloch's theorem describes electrons in periodic potentials as Bloch waves, yielding energy bands and band gaps in solids.

## Bloch's Theorem

- In a periodic potential $V(\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{R}) = V(\mathbf{r})$, energy eigenstates take the form $\psi_{n\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r}) = e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}} u_{n\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r})$, where $u$ has the lattice periodicity.
- The crystal momentum $\mathbf{k}$ is a good quantum number; $n$ labels the band.
- Eigenenergies $E_n(\mathbf{k})$ are periodic in reciprocal space, so all distinct states lie within one Brillouin zone.

## Bands and Brillouin Zones

- The Brillouin zone is the primitive cell of the reciprocal lattice; band structure is plotted along high-symmetry paths through it.
- Bragg reflection at zone boundaries opens gaps between allowed energy bands, ranges of energy with no propagating states.
- The nearly-free-electron and tight-binding pictures are complementary limits giving the same band-and-gap structure.

## Metals, Insulators, Semiconductors

- Filling bands with electrons up to the Fermi level determines conduction: a partially filled band gives a metal.
- A fully filled valence band separated by a gap from empty conduction states gives an insulator, or a semiconductor when the gap is small.
- Band theory thus explains why materials with similar atoms can be conductors or insulators.
