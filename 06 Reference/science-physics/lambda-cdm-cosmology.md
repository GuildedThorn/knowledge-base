# Lambda-CDM Cosmology

## Purpose

The Lambda-CDM model fits cosmological data with a flat expanding universe dominated by cold dark matter and dark energy.

## Core Model

- Lambda-CDM describes a homogeneous, isotropic universe via the FLRW metric, with dynamics governed by the Friedmann equations derived from general relativity.
- Lambda is the cosmological constant driving accelerated expansion (dark energy); CDM is cold (non-relativistic) dark matter that clusters to seed structure.
- A standard six-parameter fit sets baryon density, cold dark matter density, the Hubble constant, the optical depth, and the amplitude and tilt of primordial fluctuations.
- Observations imply spatial flatness, so the density components sum to the critical density.

## Key Ideas

- The Planck 2018 energy budget is roughly 5% baryons, 27% dark matter, and 68% dark energy.
- The model jointly fits the CMB, baryon acoustic oscillations, Type Ia supernova distances, and large-scale structure with few free parameters.
- A persistent Hubble tension exists: the CMB-inferred expansion rate (~67 km/s/Mpc) sits below local distance-ladder measurements (~73 km/s/Mpc).
- Lambda-CDM is phenomenologically successful but leaves the physical nature of dark matter and dark energy unexplained.
