# Maxwell's Equations and Classical Electromagnetism

## Purpose

Maxwell's four equations unify electricity and magnetism and predict self-propagating electromagnetic waves at the speed of light.

## The Four Field Equations

- Gauss's law: the divergence of E equals the charge density over the permittivity, so electric field lines originate on charges.
- Gauss's law for magnetism: the divergence of B is zero, implying no magnetic monopoles and only closed field lines.
- Faraday's law: a time-varying magnetic field induces a circulating electric field (the curl of E equals minus the rate of change of B).
- Ampere-Maxwell law: currents and a time-varying electric field (the displacement current) together produce a circulating magnetic field.

## Electromagnetic Waves

- In source-free regions the equations combine into wave equations for E and B propagating at speed c = 1/sqrt(mu0 epsilon0).
- The fields are transverse and mutually perpendicular, with E and B in phase and related by the wave impedance of free space.
- Maxwell's identification of light as an electromagnetic wave unified optics with electromagnetism.

## Gauge Potentials

- The fields derive from a scalar potential and a vector potential: B is the curl of A, and E depends on the gradient of the scalar potential and the time derivative of A.
- Gauge freedom (e.g. Lorenz or Coulomb gauge) leaves the physical fields unchanged while simplifying the equations.
- In the Lorenz gauge the potentials satisfy decoupled wave equations sourced by charge and current.
