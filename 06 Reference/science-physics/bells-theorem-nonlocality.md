# Bell's Theorem and Quantum Nonlocality

## Purpose

Bell inequalities show no local hidden-variable theory can reproduce quantum correlations, confirmed by loophole-free experiments.

## Core Model

- The EPR argument (1935) held that quantum mechanics is incomplete and that measurement outcomes are fixed by pre-existing "hidden variables" carried by each particle.
- Bell (1964) proved that any theory obeying local realism must satisfy an inequality bounding the correlations between separated measurements.
- Quantum mechanics predicts correlations that exceed this bound for entangled states, so the two frameworks are experimentally distinguishable rather than merely philosophical.

## Key Ideas

- The CHSH inequality is the standard testable form: local hidden-variable theories bound the correlator combination `S` at 2, while quantum mechanics reaches `2√2` (Tsirelson's bound).
- A conclusive test must close the locality (space-like separation), detection (fair-sampling), and freedom-of-choice loopholes simultaneously.
- Loophole-free violations were demonstrated in 2015 (Hensen/Delft with electron spins, plus photon and atom experiments), ruling out local hidden variables.
- Aspect, Clauser, and Zeilinger received the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics for the experimental program establishing these violations.
