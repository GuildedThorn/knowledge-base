# Event Horizon Telescope Black Hole Imaging

## Purpose

The Event Horizon Telescope used global VLBI to image the shadow of the supermassive black holes M87* and Sgr A*.

## How It Works

- The EHT links radio dishes across the globe into a single virtual aperture using very-long-baseline interferometry (VLBI), synthesizing a telescope the size of Earth.
- Observing at 1.3 mm (230 GHz) yields an angular resolution near 20-25 microarcseconds, enough to resolve event-horizon-scale structure.
- Atomic clocks time-stamp each station's data; petabytes are shipped on disk and correlated centrally, then imaged with regularized methods (CLEAN, RML).
- Global baselines are sparse, so image reconstruction relies on strong priors and cross-validation across independent pipelines.

## Key Ideas

- The black hole "shadow" is a central brightness depression whose size is set by the photon capture cross-section, roughly 2.6 Schwarzschild radii across.
- A bright photon ring encircles the shadow where light orbits near the unstable circular photon orbit before escaping.
- M87*, imaged in 2019, has a mass near 6.5 billion solar masses; its ~42 microarcsecond ring matched general-relativity predictions.
- Sgr A*, imaged in 2022, is ~4 million solar masses; its rapid variability made imaging far harder than the larger, steadier M87*.
