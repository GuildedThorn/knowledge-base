# General Relativity and the Einstein Field Equations

## Purpose

General relativity models gravity as spacetime curvature sourced by energy and momentum through the Einstein field equations.

## Core Model

- Spacetime geometry is encoded in the metric tensor g_{mu nu}, from which curvature is derived.
- The Einstein field equations, G_{mu nu} + Lambda g_{mu nu} = 8 pi G / c^4 T_{mu nu}, relate the Einstein curvature tensor to the stress-energy tensor.
- The stress-energy tensor T_{mu nu} describes the distribution of energy, momentum, and stress that sources curvature.
- Free-falling bodies follow geodesics, the straightest possible paths through curved spacetime.

## Limits and Solutions

- In the weak-field, slow-motion limit the equations reduce to Newtonian gravity plus small corrections.
- The Schwarzschild solution describes the vacuum spacetime around a static spherical mass and predicts black holes.
- Classic tests include light deflection, gravitational redshift, and the perihelion precession of Mercury.
- The cosmological constant Lambda enters the equations and is linked to accelerated cosmic expansion.
