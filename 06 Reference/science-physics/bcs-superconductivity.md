# BCS Theory of Superconductivity

## Purpose

BCS theory explains superconductivity through phonon-mediated Cooper pairing that opens an energy gap at the Fermi surface.

## Core Model

- Bardeen, Cooper, and Schrieffer published the microscopic theory in 1957, earning the 1972 Nobel Prize in Physics.
- Electrons attract each other weakly via lattice vibrations (phonons): one electron distorts the lattice, and a second is drawn to the resulting positive polarization.
- This attraction binds electrons of opposite momentum and spin into Cooper pairs, which behave as bosons and can condense into a single coherent ground state.
- The paired condensate is described by a many-body wavefunction with long-range phase coherence.

## Key Ideas

- Pairing opens an energy gap at the Fermi surface; unpairing a Cooper pair costs at least twice the gap energy, suppressing scattering and dissipation.
- The gap sets the critical temperature; BCS predicts a near-universal ratio between the gap and the transition temperature for weak coupling.
- The condensate expels magnetic fields (Meissner effect), the defining signature distinguishing a superconductor from a perfect conductor.
- The coherence length sets the pair size; BCS describes conventional low-temperature superconductors but not high-Tc cuprates, whose mechanism remains debated.
