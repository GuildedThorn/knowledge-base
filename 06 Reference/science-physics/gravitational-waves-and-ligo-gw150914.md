# Gravitational Waves and LIGO GW150914

## Purpose

GW150914 is the canonical observational anchor for gravitational-wave astronomy: a transient signal from two merging stellar-mass black holes detected by both Advanced LIGO interferometers on 2015-09-14.

## Core Model

- General relativity predicts accelerating asymmetric masses emit gravitational waves: spacetime strain propagating at light speed.
- Compact binary mergers produce a chirp: increasing frequency and amplitude during inspiral, followed by merger and ringdown.
- LIGO measures differential arm-length changes with laser interferometry; the relevant signal is strain, not absolute displacement.
- Matched filtering compares detector data against relativistic waveform templates to estimate source parameters and detection significance.

## GW150914 Facts

- Event time: 2015-09-14 09:50:45 UTC.
- Frequency swept from roughly 35 Hz to 250 Hz.
- Peak strain was about `1e-21`.
- Matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio was reported as 24.
- The source was a binary black-hole merger at about 410 Mpc luminosity distance.
- Source-frame component masses were about 36 and 29 solar masses; final black hole mass about 62 solar masses.
- Around 3 solar masses of energy equivalent were radiated as gravitational waves.

## Engineering / Measurement Notes

- Coincidence across geographically separated detectors is essential; a single interferometer transient is not enough.
- Detector calibration, environmental monitors, vetoes, and statistical background estimation are part of the scientific claim.
- The waveform encodes both astrophysics and tests of general relativity: inspiral probes orbital dynamics, ringdown probes final black-hole modes.
- The result converted gravitational-wave detection from indirect inference to direct measurement.
