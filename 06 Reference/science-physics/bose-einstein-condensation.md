# Bose-Einstein Condensation

## Purpose

Below a critical temperature bosons macroscopically occupy the ground state, first realized in dilute ultracold atomic gases.

## Core Physics

- Bose-Einstein statistics allow unlimited occupation of a single quantum state; below a critical temperature a macroscopic fraction of atoms condense into the lowest-energy mode.
- The transition occurs when the thermal de Broglie wavelength becomes comparable to the interparticle spacing, so wavefunctions overlap and phase coherence sets in.
- For an ideal uniform Bose gas $T_c \propto n^{2/3}/m$, where $n$ is number density and $m$ the atomic mass.
- The condensate is described by a single macroscopic wavefunction, giving long-range phase coherence and, with interactions, superfluid behavior.

## Experimental Realization

- Anderson et al. (1995) produced a BEC in dilute rubidium-87 vapor by laser cooling followed by evaporative cooling in a magnetic trap, reaching ~170 nK.
- Wieman, Cornell (rubidium), and Ketterle (sodium) shared the 2001 Nobel Prize for these dilute-gas condensates.
- Dilute atomic BECs are weakly interacting, so mean-field Gross-Pitaevskii theory describes them well, unlike strongly interacting superfluid helium-4.
