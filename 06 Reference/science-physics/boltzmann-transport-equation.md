# The Boltzmann Transport Equation

## Purpose

The Boltzmann equation governs the evolution of a gas's distribution function and underlies irreversibility and transport.

## Distribution Function and Collision Integral

- The single-particle distribution f(r, v, t) counts particles in a phase-space cell; the equation tracks its evolution.
- Streaming terms describe free flight and the action of external forces on the phase-space density.
- The collision integral encodes binary collisions that redistribute velocities, derived under the molecular-chaos (Stosszahlansatz) assumption.
- The nonlinear collision term makes the equation an integro-differential equation that is generally hard to solve exactly.

## H-Theorem and Irreversibility

- Boltzmann's H-function, essentially the negative of entropy, decreases monotonically until equilibrium is reached.
- The H-theorem gives a microscopic route to the second law and singles out the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution as the stationary state.
- The apparent conflict between reversible microdynamics and irreversible H-decay is resolved by the statistical molecular-chaos assumption (Loschmidt and recurrence objections).

## Transport Coefficients

- Near-equilibrium solutions (Chapman-Enskog expansion) yield viscosity, thermal conductivity, and diffusion coefficients from molecular parameters.
- The relaxation-time (BGK) approximation replaces the full collision integral with relaxation toward local equilibrium.
- The framework extends to electron transport in solids, neutron transport, and radiative transfer.
