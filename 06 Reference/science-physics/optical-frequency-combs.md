# Optical Frequency Combs

## Purpose

Mode-locked optical frequency combs act as rulers linking optical and microwave frequencies, revolutionizing precision metrology.

## Mode-Locked Pulse Trains

- A mode-locked laser emits a periodic train of ultrashort pulses whose spectrum is a comb of equally spaced sharp lines.
- Each comb line frequency is f = n*f_rep + f_ceo, where f_rep is the pulse repetition rate and f_ceo is the carrier-envelope offset.
- The repetition rate sets the comb tooth spacing and lies in the microwave range, providing the optical-to-microwave link.
- Broadening in nonlinear fiber can widen the comb to an octave, essential for self-referencing.

## Carrier-Envelope Offset Stabilization

- f_ceo arises because the carrier wave slips relative to the pulse envelope from pulse to pulse.
- The f-2f self-referencing technique doubles the low-frequency comb end and beats it against the high-frequency end to measure f_ceo directly.
- Locking both f_rep and f_ceo to a stable reference fixes every comb line to a known absolute frequency.

## Optical Frequency Metrology

- Combs let optical clock transitions be counted directly, enabling atomic clocks far more stable than microwave standards.
- Applications include precision spectroscopy, astronomical spectrograph calibration, and low-noise microwave generation.
- The technique earned the 2005 Nobel Prize in Physics for Hall and Haensch.
