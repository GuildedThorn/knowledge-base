# The Fluctuation-Dissipation Theorem

## Purpose

The fluctuation-dissipation theorem links a system's equilibrium fluctuations to its linear response and dissipation.

## Linear Response and Susceptibility

- A small external perturbation shifts an observable by an amount set by the generalized susceptibility (response function).
- The imaginary part of the frequency-dependent susceptibility quantifies energy absorption and dissipation.
- Causality constrains the response function through the Kramers-Kronig relations linking its real and imaginary parts.

## Correlation-Response Relation

- The theorem equates the equilibrium correlation (spectrum) of spontaneous fluctuations to the dissipative part of the response, scaled by temperature.
- In the classical limit the fluctuation spectrum is proportional to kT times the imaginary susceptibility over frequency.
- It shows that the same microscopic mechanism causing random fluctuations also causes dissipation of imposed motion (e.g. drag on a Brownian particle).
- Callen and Welton generalized earlier special cases into one broadly applicable relation.

## Johnson-Nyquist Noise

- Thermal agitation of charge carriers produces voltage noise across a resistor with spectral density set by 4*k*T*R.
- This Johnson-Nyquist noise is the canonical example: resistance (dissipation) and voltage fluctuations are two faces of one process.
- The result sets fundamental noise floors for amplifiers, detectors, and precision measurements.
