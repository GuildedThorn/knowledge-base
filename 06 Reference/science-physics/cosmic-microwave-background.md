# The Cosmic Microwave Background

## Purpose

The CMB is the relic blackbody radiation from recombination whose tiny anisotropies encode the early universe.

## Core Physics

- The CMB is a near-perfect blackbody at 2.725 K, released when the universe cooled enough for electrons and protons to combine into neutral hydrogen (recombination) at redshift ~1100.
- Before recombination, photons scattered constantly off free electrons; afterward the universe became transparent and photons free-streamed, forming the surface of last scattering.
- Penzias and Wilson detected the CMB in 1964 as an unexplained microwave excess, confirming a hot Big Bang origin over steady-state models.
- COBE's FIRAS instrument confirmed the blackbody spectrum to high precision, earning the 2006 Nobel Prize.

## Anisotropies

- Temperature fluctuations are tiny, about one part in 100,000, superimposed on the uniform background.
- These anisotropies are dominated by a dipole from Earth's motion, with intrinsic fluctuations analyzed via their angular power spectrum.
- Acoustic peaks in the power spectrum reflect sound waves in the pre-recombination photon-baryon plasma; peak positions and heights constrain curvature, baryon density, and dark matter.
- WMAP and Planck mapped the anisotropies precisely, anchoring the Lambda-CDM parameter fit.
