# ITER Tokamak Fusion Physics Basis

## Purpose

ITER is a tokamak fusion experiment whose design depends on extrapolating plasma physics from existing magnetic-confinement experiments to burning-plasma scale.

## Core Model

- A tokamak confines plasma with a combination of toroidal magnetic field, poloidal field from plasma current, and shaping/control coils.
- Deuterium-tritium fusion produces alpha particles; alpha heating is central to burning-plasma behavior.
- Performance depends on confinement, density and pressure limits, current drive, heating, impurity control, MHD stability, disruptions, and plasma-wall interaction.
- The divertor and plasma-facing components must exhaust heat and particles while limiting impurity influx and material damage.

## Physics Basis Areas

- Core confinement and transport: how heat, particles, and momentum are retained or lost.
- Edge and divertor plasma: scrape-off-layer transport, detachment, erosion, tritium retention, and heat-load management.
- MHD stability: pressure/current limits, instabilities, disruptions, and operational boundaries.
- Energetic particles: alpha-particle confinement, wave-particle interactions, and loss mechanisms.
- Plasma operation and control: scenario development, diagnostics, actuator control, and safe shutdown.

## Engineering Notes

- ITER is not just "make plasma hot"; the hard problem is maintaining a controlled burning plasma while protecting material surfaces.
- Scaling laws are useful but risky; extrapolation from smaller tokamaks must be backed by modeling, diagnostics, and operational margins.
- Divertor physics is an availability and component-lifetime issue, not just a plasma-performance detail.
- Burning-plasma systems couple physics and control loops tightly: heating, confinement, impurity content, and stability interact.
