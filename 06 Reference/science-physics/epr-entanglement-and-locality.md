# EPR, Entanglement, and Locality

## Purpose

The Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen paper is a foundational argument about whether quantum mechanics is a complete description of physical reality when spatially separated systems have strongly correlated measurement outcomes.

## Core Model

- EPR considers two systems prepared so measurements on one allow prediction of corresponding properties of the other.
- The argument assumes a locality principle: measuring one separated system should not physically disturb the other.
- It also uses a reality criterion: if a quantity can be predicted with certainty without disturbance, it corresponds to an element of reality.
- Under those assumptions, EPR argues the wavefunction is not a complete description.

## Historical Impact

- Bohr replied by challenging how EPR framed measurement and physical reality.
- Bell later turned the philosophical tension into testable inequalities.
- Experiments violating Bell inequalities shifted the live issue from "hidden variables in general" to the much narrower compatibility of hidden-variable theories with locality.
- Modern quantum information treats entanglement as a resource, not just a conceptual problem.

## Engineering / Conceptual Notes

- Entanglement correlation does not imply usable faster-than-light communication.
- The important distinction is between correlation structure, measurement choice, and signal transmission.
- Quantum computing and quantum networking discussions should separate state preparation, measurement basis, decoherence, and classical side-channel communication.
- EPR is useful background for quantum error correction, quantum cryptography, and device-independent protocols.
