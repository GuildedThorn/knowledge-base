# Laser Physics and Stimulated Emission

## Purpose

Lasers produce coherent light by stimulated emission amplified in an optical resonator with a population-inverted gain medium.

## Stimulated Emission and Population Inversion

- An incident photon can stimulate an excited atom to emit a second photon identical in phase, frequency, direction, and polarization.
- Net amplification requires population inversion: more atoms in the upper state than the lower, contrary to thermal equilibrium.
- Inversion is created by pumping (optical, electrical, or chemical) and typically needs three- or four-level energy schemes to sustain.
- The Einstein A and B coefficients relate spontaneous emission, stimulated emission, and absorption rates.

## Optical Resonators

- Two mirrors form a cavity that feeds photons back through the gain medium for repeated amplification.
- Lasing begins when round-trip gain exceeds round-trip losses (the threshold condition).
- The cavity selects discrete longitudinal and transverse modes set by its length and geometry.
- One partially reflective output coupler lets a fraction of the intracavity power exit as the beam.

## Gain and Coherence

- Output light is highly monochromatic, spatially and temporally coherent, and strongly directional.
- Gain saturation clamps the steady-state intensity as stimulated emission depletes the inversion.
- Coherence properties underpin holography, interferometry, and precision spectroscopy.
