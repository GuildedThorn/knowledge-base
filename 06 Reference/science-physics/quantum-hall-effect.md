# The Quantum Hall Effect

## Purpose

In strong magnetic fields the Hall conductance of a 2D electron gas is quantized in exact universal units.

## Core Physics

- A two-dimensional electron gas in a strong perpendicular magnetic field has its energy quantized into discrete, highly degenerate Landau levels.
- The Hall conductance is quantized in units of e-squared over Planck's constant, forming flat plateaus as the field or density varies.
- Von Klitzing discovered the integer quantum Hall effect in 1980, winning the 1985 Nobel Prize; the quantization is exact to parts per billion regardless of sample details.
- On a plateau the longitudinal resistance vanishes, indicating dissipationless edge transport.

## Key Ideas

- The quantization is topological: the Hall conductance equals an integer Chern number, robust against disorder and imperfections.
- Current flows in one-dimensional chiral edge channels immune to backscattering, explaining the vanishing longitudinal resistance.
- The precision made the effect a metrology standard; the von Klitzing constant now helps define the ohm and the kilogram via fixed constants.
- The fractional quantum Hall effect, discovered in cleaner samples, shows plateaus at fractional filling from strong electron correlations and hosts fractionally charged quasiparticles (1998 Nobel Prize).
